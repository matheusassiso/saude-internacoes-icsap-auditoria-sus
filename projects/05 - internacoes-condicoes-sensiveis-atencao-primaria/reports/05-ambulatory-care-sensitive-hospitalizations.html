Ambulatory Care Sensitive Hospitalizations in Mato Grosso do Sul

SIH/SUS records and Brazilian ICSAP classification, 2024

Matheus Assis de Oliveira

June 26, 2026

Abstract

This article presents a reproducible analysis of ambulatory care sensitive hospitalizations in Mato Grosso do Sul using SIH/SUS hospital authorization records from 2024. The project applies the Brazilian ICSAP list published by Portaria SAS/MS no. 221/2008 to the main ICD-10 diagnosis field, filters records to MS residents, joins IBGE municipal population estimates, and calculates crude municipal rates per 10,000 residents. The final processed dataset contains 213,411 hospitalizations for MS residents, of which 37,750 are classified as ICSAP. The crude statewide rate is 130.1 per 10,000 residents, and ICSAP records represent 17.7% of hospitalizations. Results are screening indicators and should not be interpreted as direct causal evidence of primary-care quality.

Introduction

Ambulatory care sensitive hospitalizations are admissions for conditions that may often be prevented, controlled, or reduced through timely and effective primary care. They are widely used as health-system screening indicators because they connect hospital outcomes with prevention, chronic disease management, access, and continuity of care.

In Brazil, the official reference is the Brazilian list of Internacoes por Condicoes Sensiveis a Atencao Primaria, published by Portaria SAS/MS no. 221/2008. The list groups ICD-10 diagnoses into 19 cause groups.

This project asks:

Which municipalities and diagnosis groups concentrate ambulatory care sensitive hospitalizations in Mato Grosso do Sul?

The analysis is descriptive. A high municipal rate can reflect primary-care issues, but it can also reflect age structure, disease burden, hospital access, coding practices, referral flows, or small-population instability.

Data

The hospital data come from SIH/SUS reduced AIH files (RD) for Mato Grosso do Sul in 2024. The analysis uses municipality of residence, not municipality of hospitalization, so the indicator is aligned with resident population denominators.

Methods

Classification

The helper script standardizes the main diagnosis field and applies the Brazilian ICSAP ICD-10 groups. Each hospitalization receives:

is_icsap = TRUE/FALSE
icsap_group = 1 to 19 when applicable
group_label = readable group label

Indicators

For municipality \(i\), the crude ICSAP rate is:

\[ Rate_i = \frac{ICSAP_i}{Population_i} \times 10,000 \]

The municipal ICSAP share is:

\[ Share_i = \frac{ICSAP_i}{Hospitalizations_i} \]

The mortality rate among ICSAP hospitalizations is:

\[ Mortality_i = \frac{Deaths^{ICSAP}_i}{ICSAP_i} \times 100 \]

These indicators are crude and not age-standardized.

Monthly Pattern

The monthly series reflects care demand, hospital flows, seasonality, and recording dynamics. A one-year monthly view is useful for description but not enough to infer stable seasonality.

Spatial Pattern

The map is a screening surface. It should guide questions, not conclusions. Municipal context, age structure, service network, and primary-care coverage must be added before interpreting local performance.

Discussion

The statewide ICSAP burden is substantial: 37,750 hospitalizations and R$ 59,931,019 in approved value in 2024. The leading groups include kidney and urinary tract infections, gastroenteritis, cerebrovascular diseases, pulmonary diseases, heart failure, bacterial pneumonias, and diabetes. This profile suggests that preventable or primary-care-sensitive hospitalizations are not limited to one clinical area; they span infections, chronic disease complications, respiratory disease, and cardiovascular conditions.

Municipal crude rates vary sharply. This is expected in a state with small municipalities and uneven hospital flows. The indicator is strongest when used as a first-pass screening metric, especially when paired with age standardization, multi-year averages, primary-care structure, and local validation.

Limitations

Important limitations:

  • ICSAP is a screening indicator, not direct proof of poor primary care.
  • Rates are crude and not age-standardized.
  • Small municipalities can have unstable rates.
  • SIH/SUS does not include private admissions outside SUS financing.
  • Main diagnosis coding can vary across providers.
  • Hospital access and referral flows affect hospitalization counts.
  • One year is not enough to infer trend or persistent outlier status.

Reproducibility

The helper script builds all outputs:

source("src/R/icsap_sih.R")
outputs <- build_icsap_outputs(year = 2024)

rmarkdown::render(

Conclusion

S05 transforms SIH/SUS records into a municipality-level ICSAP monitoring panel for Mato Grosso do Sul. It provides a reproducible ICD-10 classification, municipal crude rates, diagnosis-group summaries, monthly patterns, and a map layer. The project is useful as a portfolio example because it combines public health concepts, microdata processing, population denominators, and cautious interpretation.
